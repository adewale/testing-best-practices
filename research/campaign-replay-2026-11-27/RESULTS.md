## Comparison with OpenClaw

[OpenClaw's pinned Test Audit](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/tree/9fd0b7de19b70c97a285d5ff4c852d66f952190e/.agents/skills/test-audit) is a substantive comparator: inspect the complete candidate and its owner, callers, siblings, history and CI; choose a few high-confidence changes; preserve independent contracts. Its whole-subsystem campaign adds exhaustive declaration/parameter accounting, owner lanes, a second layer-level pass, boundary preservation review and reconciliation of upstream additions. This study tests the bounded ordinary path. It cannot rank the full multi-reviewer campaign.

The revised skill adopts explicit inventory/availability/read/run reporting and complete contract accounting when retiring a layer. Its additional emphasis is paired old/keeper faults, permitted-change controls, provider-grounded fixtures, checker calibration and measurement compatibility. The tool fixes make those requirements executable for specified development fixtures. OpenClaw's native timing, line budgets and commands are repository-specific and should be mapped to the target rather than copied as arbitrary targets.

## Limits

The repositories and known anchors were purposively selected; they are not unseen generalization holdouts. There is one subject per cell and one same-family judge per case. Guidance can be inferred from prose. Subject isolation is instructional on a shared host, and neither complete read/tool traces, provider-attested identities nor token costs are available. Native controls run in bounded offline containers; source-only and unavailable evidence remain distinct. Two environment events affected four execution attempts: three missing reports and one delivered report with unavailable backend completion. A separate failed judge leaves Keyboardia without an accepted blind semantic judgment. These results support specific observed decisions and repairs, not a population reliability estimate or a claim that the revised skill is superior.

## Improvements made from this replay

The post-replay guidance requires both public acceptance and public rejection even when expensive negative fixtures move to a checker. E79 turns the observed miss into executable positive, false-acceptance and false-rejection controls. It was not rerun through model subjects.

The skill now also distinguishes metric calibration from model improvement when an answer format changes; requires reachable setups that expose one guard while allowing other guards through; accepts alternate repairs under the same contract/fault/allowed-change/keeper bar; and records checkpoint artifacts separately from agent completion. It warns against shared writable dependency links. These additions follow the observed failures rather than converting suite size, mutation scores or assertion counts into targets.

To establish a stronger comparative result, the next study would need unseen repositories and anchors, repeated subjects, independently isolated dependency trees, centrally recorded reads and commands, and judges with measured agreement. Keep the failed attempts and unchanged campaign criteria. Compare the complete OpenClaw campaign only with a similarly resourced exhaustive audit, separately from the ordinary bounded path tested here. Those are future study requirements, not evidence already obtained.
